# The material door

The owner hands over an article, a doc, a body of someone's public writing, or another skill, and names the artifact it belongs in. The destination is already known, so the only question is what the material changes about that artifact.

## Read it whole first

Material the request is built on is read whole, in the main thread, whatever the count. It is the spec, and the judgment worth transplanting lives in connective tissue that excerpting removes — the aside that qualifies the rule, the example that shows where the rule stops. A trailing list of secondary links may fan out to one reader per source under a quote-only contract, but the primary material is not summarised before it is understood.

Seek what the material doesn't cover. Where the subject has current practice beyond it, go find that and name the sources you added separately from the ones you were given. Verify anything fast-moving or version-specific against a freshly retrieved canonical source at the time of writing; recall ships deprecated specifics in a confident tone.

## Then ask what it actually changes

Three outcomes, and two of them are not an edit.

- **It restates what the artifact already encodes.** Say so and change nothing. Working material in for its own sake is how a body doubles in size without steering any differently.
- **It contradicts what the artifact encodes.** This is the valuable case and it is a decision, not an edit. State the contradiction with the evidence from both sides and ask: follow the new material, keep the artifact as it stands, hear the owner's reasoning, or another path. Her domain expertise is evidence of a kind the source doesn't carry, and "away with your opinion" is a complete answer.
- **It adds judgment the artifact lacks.** Work it in as a delta at the place it belongs, which is usually inside an existing section rather than beside it.

## Rework, never append

The artifact must read as one voice afterward. Distil the material into the artifact's own structure and vocabulary, keeping the judgment it carries and discarding its shape.

- **No summary-of-the-source section.** A section that reads as a digest of an article is the signature of appending rather than integrating. If the new content cannot find a home inside the existing structure, the structure is the thing to reconsider.
- **No dependency on the source.** The artifact never cites the material as its authority, never tells the agent to go read it, and never assumes it is reachable. Canonical documentation the agent can verify against mid-task is the one exception, because that directs action. Everything else — where the idea came from, whose writing it was, why it was persuasive — goes to CHANGELOG.md.
- **When the material is another skill, take the ideas and leave the wiring.** Rework them into this artifact's voice. Create no cross-reference, no shared file, and no back-reference wired into the source: coupling two artifacts so each leans on the other breaks both the moment one is missing. Never edit the source artifact as a side effect of ingesting it.
- **One term per concept.** Material arrives with its author's vocabulary. Two words for one idea, inherited from two sources, reads as two ideas.
- **Cut what the model would do without.** The bar is not "is this true" but "does the agent behave differently for having read it". Interesting-but-unproven material is the most expensive thing to encode, because it pays rent until something forces its removal.

## Before applying

State what class of work the addition changes and how. If that can't be stated, the material was interesting rather than operational, and the honest outcome is no change plus a note in the changelog that it was read and declined.

Then write the preservation set, as every path through this artifact does: what the target currently does well that the addition must not disturb. New material displacing working guidance is the same regression mode as a bad fix, arriving through a friendlier door.
